## Current disposition

Subscriptions owns accepted customer terms, lifecycle decisions, commercial revisions, durable operations and money-free commercial facts. Entitlements owns applying commercial posture to quotas, licenses and resources, including adapter contracts and reconciliation. Pricing stores immutable commercial rules; Rating values accepted facts; Billing owns financial allocation, posting and correction.

Supported changes are immediate quantity increases and component additions. Orders future starts, cancellation schedules, normal renewal/trial timers and guarded early trial conversion remain supported under their capability gates. Separate customer acceptance confirmation, ramps, co-termination, collection pause, notification delivery, tenant/payer transfer, administrative historical correction, import, trial extension/repeat, standalone acquisition, scheduled composition/resume, linked subscription replacement and general base-plan changes are outside the current scope. Unsupported commands refuse before effects, including through operator wrappers.

Missing required notice or acceptance cannot be treated as satisfied; terms that need one are refused at acceptance instead (SUB-D-69). Legacy-system migration is not required.

Operational provenance, immutable history, authorization and recoverable domain events remain required. Dedicated audit functionality is outside the current scope. Current-system replay and Rating/Billing corrections remain required.

## Lifecycle and commercial records

**SUB-D-01: Scheduled lifecycle intents.** Cancellation supports immediate, end-of-term and explicit-date modes. Pending intents are durable. They can be withdrawn before application and are evaluated by the normal guards at their boundary. Scheduling is planning evidence, not applied service or a billable fact. Scheduled composition and resume are unsupported.

**SUB-D-02: Quantity changes.** Quantity increases and component additions are first-class transitions. One request has one expected revision, one effective instant and one atomic resulting composition. Existing lots retain their terms; the delta receives its own immutable accepted terms and purchase provenance. Decreases and removals are unsupported.

**SUB-D-03: Collection pause scope.** Collection pause is unsupported in the current profile.

**SUB-D-04: Ramp scope.** Contract-authored multi-step ramps are unsupported in the current profile.

**SUB-D-05: Activation dates.** Contract booking and the caller's requested start are distinct from actual service activation. Subscriptions records actual activation T locally after initial readiness and final guards. The subscription is `preparing` during preparation (SUB-D-58). A profile requiring separate customer acceptance cannot launch without that capability.

**SUB-D-06: Immutable ordering identity.** `orderingTenantId` is fixed at creation and supplies the subscription stream/partition identity. Creation-time commercial tenant axes remain explicit; tenant/payer transfer is unsupported.

**SUB-D-07: Commercial and monetary ownership.** Subscriptions publishes money-free recurring, usage-parent and qualifying one-time facts. Rating values them from frozen accepted terms. Billing applies the complete financial result, existing advances and final invoice rounding/corrections. Replay does not create another obligation or collection.

**SUB-D-08: One mutation path.** Every supported mutation, including create, renewal, intent withdrawal and archive, uses the durable operation envelope and local commit path. Creation has tenant-scoped business deduplication and starts the revision stream. Unsupported operations cannot enter through alternate administrative endpoints.

**SUB-D-09: Event authority.** Subscriptions owns its lifecycle, intent and commercial-fact events. State, operation outcome and publication intent commit atomically. Provider execution observations do not become a second source of commercial decisions.

**SUB-D-11: Draft cancellation.** A draft can be cancelled through the ordinary guarded operation. An unconfirmed draft has no automatic occupancy claim; its configurable housekeeping TTL has no financial effect. Protected purchase claims and unresolved preparation/payment effects survive draft cleanup and require their own fenced resolution.

**SUB-D-12: Renewal collection.** Collection-pause renewal bypass is unsupported; renewal retains its required financial guards.

**SUB-D-13: Term boundaries.** `autoRenew = false` reaches the normal end-of-term cancellation route. Renewal recovery advances an agreed term occurrence at most once and retains its accepted boundary and billing posture. A payment outcome alone does not resume service or revive a terminal subscription. [Renewal and offboarding](features/04-renewal-retention.md#7-renewal-and-offboarding-integration-contract) defines the applicable late-success and suspension cases.

**SUB-D-15: Manual change scope.** Immediate supported purchases use the ordinary atomic composition path. No ramp schedule or ramp-override operation is enabled.

**SUB-D-16: Nonpayment dwell.** Take the dwell policy from the default set frozen with the current term, and snapshot its provenance and deadline when the suspension episode starts. Configuration changes do not move that running deadline. The platform default is 90 days. Expiry submits guarded cancellation; only applied cancellation releases the subscription's occupancy claim.

**SUB-D-17: Required notice.** Renewal or continuation must satisfy the notice/assent prerequisites of its accepted terms. A price successor is not permission to amend those terms. Notification delivery is deferred, so terms that require a notice are refused at acceptance (SUB-D-69) instead of blocking an action at runtime.

**SUB-D-18: Cancellation money boundary.** Cancellation updates commercial obligations through immutable successor facts and seals. Subscriptions computes no refund, credit or early-termination amount. Billing handles financial correction and settlement under accepted terms and explicit cutoff evidence.

**SUB-D-19: Per-component facts.** A commercial obligation includes the component/interval identity within its subscription and period. Distinct component obligations cannot collide under one subscription-wide key. Rating may derive multiple calculation windows or lines from one fact.

**SUB-D-20: As-of attribution.** Facts describe the effective composition, quantities, payer, terms and served intervals of their economic time. Processing delay must not substitute current catalog data or current attribution. Complete historical evidence remains recoverable independently of event delivery.

**SUB-D-21: Component and fact identity.** Component intervals retain stable identities. Mid-period additions and terminal boundaries publish the complete affected commercial obligations, including earlier served intervals. Same-obligation quantity changes publish full successor versions; they do not create an independent monetary delta. Trial obligations retain their accepted terms and actual episode boundaries.

**SUB-D-22: Dwell lifecycle.** Authoritative payment resolution clears only the blocking dwell episode; a committed exit from suspension clears its applicable dwell. An authoritative dispute can hold the episode's clock under the selected policy. Retry recovers the same cancellation operation and recorded resource outcome. No pending cancellation, timer or timeout releases a retained place.

**SUB-D-23: Intent firing failures.** Transient dependency uncertainty retries with bounded backoff and retained suppressions. Definitive refusal becomes an explicit failed intent. If a state precondition is expected to clear, such as conversion while suspended, park the intent and pause its firing deadline until the unblocking transition. A terminal lifecycle outcome settles the pending work.

**SUB-D-24: One-time valuation.** A qualifying agreed action produces an amount-less fact valued by Rating from immutable accepted terms. Billing owns financial application. An advance is applied to the same obligation and cannot be collected again at activation.

**SUB-D-25: Reason-aware cancellation.** Cancellation carries its reason, effective cutoff, term and period evidence. Customer/operator early termination can be eligible for contractual treatment; natural term expiry and nonpayment exhaustion do not invent an early-termination fee. Exact financial consequences remain governed by accepted terms and Billing's contract.

**SUB-D-27: Billing calendar.** Subscriptions derives period identity from the frozen `billingAnchorPolicy` and anchor. A month-end clamp does not drift: January 31 reaches February 28/29 and then March 31. Rating uses the same geometry without independently creating a second recurring obligation.

**SUB-D-28: Chargeable-action occurrence.** Each separately agreed chargeable business action has one durable occurrence identity independent of prices and technical attempts. Retry, resume, trial conversion, cancellation/re-add, catalog change and reprovision do not create a fee by themselves. Correcting the original action retains its occurrence and publishes a successor fact/seal. Reserving an ID, prepaying or preparing resources does not prove the action occurred.

**SUB-D-29: PriceBook bindings.** Accepted bindings retain price IDs, revision/item identity, selected dimensions, product evidence and policy/invoice terms. Publishing a price revision does not change existing purchases. Renewal resolves under accepted renewal policy; an in-term binding's own `ends_on` requires explicit continuation authority. `effective_to` alone does not terminate a pin. Replay reads pinned immutable money.

## Ownership and recovery

**SUB-D-30: Commercial posture.** Subscriptions records `CommercialPostureV1` with the source revision and outbox. Entitlements applies it through provider-specific capabilities. A local subscription receipt does not prove applied provider access; initial activation consumes the readiness evidence defined by SUB-D-55–57.

**SUB-D-31: Pricing adapter.** The [Pricing integration](DESIGN.md#332-pricing-acceptance-and-accepted-time) uses the read, acceptance and sellability ports. An eligibility observation is not a durable purchase commitment. Accepted commercial evidence is separate from Rating calculation snapshots. Future-purchase and trial extensions follow the [accepted-acquisition](DESIGN.md#accepted-acquisition-evidence) and [trial-evidence](DESIGN.md#336-trial-terms-and-provider-evidence) contracts. The [foundation](features/01-foundation.md#acquisition-recovery) and [trial](features/06-trials.md#credit-preparation-and-recovery) features define recovery.

**SUB-D-32: Commercial fact authority.** Subscriptions owns immutable full fact versions, complete payer-specific commercial membership and exact seals. Usage parents open with the commercial period; Rating owns child-window work. Full-state attribution segments, facts, membership and source checkpoints are recoverable by pull. Commercial completeness, usage-source coverage and Rating calculation completeness are independent. Removal publishes explicit zero-obligation evidence; it does not erase history.

**SUB-D-33: Durable operation recovery.** Acceptance, local state and outbox commit together. Business create identity is `(originType, originId, originVersion, attemptId, lineId)`; for Orders it includes order/version/attempt/line, but not receiver generation. Neither a new transport key nor a new generation can create another draft for that identity. Settle unknown create under the same serialization boundary as create; negative settlement leaves a durable no-create tombstone covering all generations of that attempt. The reservation uses the separate origin/version/line key and its own release tombstone; attempt settlement does not end the reservation. Acceptance is distinct from terminal application. Unknown external effects retain recovery and cannot be discarded by expiry, withdrawal or compensation. Remote calls run outside the local commit lock; application revalidates local fences.

Technical retries retain the original attempt. A successor attempt requires proven predecessor non-application, safe effect settlement/quarantine, financial recovery and exclusive reassociation to the same valid acquisition. Preserve the existing claim, payment/commitment, charge occurrence and predecessor lineage. Applied predecessors are recovered. A changed order version is a commercial amendment, with explicit claim/commitment mapping and financial applicability; it is not a technical retry or automatic cross-payer funding transfer.

**SUB-D-34: Atomic composition.** Validate the entire resulting composition against one expected revision and effective instant, self-exempting the subscription's existing occupancy. Purchase retries create no second lot. Commit composition, lots, operation result, affected full facts, successor seal and outbox atomically. Unsupported multi-date changes refuse. [Changes](features/03-changes.md#apply-one-atomic-changecomposition) defines the procedure.

**SUB-D-35: Resource disposition.** Suspension preserves data-bearing resources. Cancellation separates confirmed charging cutoff, applied commercial cancellation, actual resource stop and deletion. Ordinary customer accrual ends at the confirmed cutoff despite delayed stop, or at an authoritative earlier offboarding stop. Actual runtime remains truthful. Retained data alone creates no fee; separately agreed retention terms may create an obligation.

Cancellation confirmation requires authorization, commercial eligibility and durable cutoff recording. A request receipt is insufficient. Withdrawal/rescheduling requires an explicit versioned confirmed action before cutoff and cannot restore past charges. After cutoff, service requires a valid guarded route. Platform-fault/maintenance stops beyond the accepted SLA end ordinary accrual at authoritative stop; operator/abuse/policy stops follow the accepted suspension billing posture and do not create a cutoff by themselves.

Explicitly bound agreed terms take precedence over versioned configuration, which is the policy set resolved from Settings Service by platform and seller-tenant scope (SUB-D-72). Freeze the selected values and deadlines. The [current platform defaults](PRD.md#platform-defaults-and-contract-binding) are 7-day grace, 90-day nonpayment dwell, 30-day retention and 90-day archive dwell with completed data disposition. Retention notices have 7-day and 1-day lead times; their scheduling and delivery are deferred, and the v1 default retention does not require them, so their absence does not block deletion (SUB-D-69). Hold prevents purge. Final hold release creates a new recorded generation/deadline. A Contract ending mid-term leaves the running term unchanged; next renewal uses an explicitly bound successor or the applicable platform defaults, which carry no notice requirement in this version (SUB-D-69).

Financial evidence retention is separate from resource-data retention.

**SUB-D-36: Trials.** Pricing PlanRevision carries an optional free-period block with explicit zero prices OR a monetary-credit block covering ordinary tariff value. Products owns SKU identity/structure; Billing owns grants, allocations and corrections; Rating retains gross valuation; Subscriptions owns episode boundaries and conversion. Paid conversion follows the accepted settlement scheme: confirmed prescribed prepayment or durable authorization to serve on credit. A card is not universally required. Unknown financial evidence grants no permission.

Definitive prepay failure causes no paid start or waiting-gap debt; normal expiry retains data, and later paid service needs a supported guarded route.

Credit trials require one operation-bound Billing grant commitment before preparation. Successful local T makes that same grant effective; later Billing recording is recovery, not a new access gate. Pre-T preparation consumes no bonus. Eligible residual before component `B` is platform-borne, including the hourly remainder and late positive corrections; residual from `B` follows paid terms regardless of arrival time (SUB-D-67). Original grant expiry stays exact; expiry without conversion creates no paid boundary.

Full Rating corrections adjust the same grant once. Original expiry, restrictions and result lineage remain. After expiry, corrected accounting creates no spendable credit. Correction cannot reopen an exhausted/closed episode or reverse an applied paid conversion. Early guarded conversion preserves eligible unused credit on the same grant until original expiry. Concurrent initial trial acquisition is serialized by payer/plan; served-episode history prevents repeat trials across new subscription IDs. Extension, repeat, regrant, stacking, transfer and reactivation are unsupported.

Free-period delivery does not require a promotional-grant provider; credit delivery does.

**SUB-D-38: Documentation authority.** PRD owns observable requirements; DESIGN owns architecture; features own procedures and acceptance; DECOMPOSITION assigns each active FR/NFR one primary feature; UPSTREAM_REQS records dependency evidence; this register owns decisions. [UPSTREAM_REQS](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#25-provider-prerequisites-and-bilateral-acceptance) distinguishes required provider contracts from owner adoption and measured conformance.

**SUB-D-39: Suspended purchase refusal.** Quantity increases, seat purchases and component additions refuse while suspended at both submission and commit. Perform a separate authorized guarded resume before the normal active change. Purchase does not resume service or queue itself. If suspension wins, refuse the unapplied purchase and recover staged effects; if purchase applied first, retain its obligations and reconcile remaining delivery under the later suspension fence. Draft changes retain their separate S-U01 gate.

Renewal recovery and unrelated administration retain their own contracts.

## Commercial valuation

**SUB-D-40: Exact commercial seals.** Every immutable seal names exact `(fact_id, fact_version, payload_digest)` members. Billing accepts only complete results matching those entries. Changed commercial content/membership needs a successor seal. A newer Rating calculation for the same exact fact may produce a successor financial target under the existing seal. Commercial-head freshness, complete manifests, invoice-version fencing and unknown predecessor recovery still apply.

**SUB-D-41: Authorized continuation.** Automatic in-term continuation at `ends_on` requires explicitly accepted policy and satisfied notice/assent. Pricing selects a permissible successor; Subscriptions authorizes and publishes its full fact and exact seal atomically. Rating cannot substitute terms. Missing authority or covered pricing leaves full valuation pending; the known prefix is not a complete result. No implicit debt, free-service or shutdown policy follows.

**SUB-D-42: Explicit unresolved interval.** An established obligation with a known boundary but no authorized continuation publishes a full successor fact with an explicit `Unresolved` interval and successor exact seal. Keep known intervals and lot/quantity/purchase lineage. Rating advances the source head but leaves full valuation pending; Billing cannot substitute an older result or infer automatic reversal. Resolution creates another full fact/seal. Implicit gaps and unpriced new admission are invalid.

**SUB-D-43: One-time calculation basis.** `Once` means a flat action fee; `Units { quantity, unit }` freezes the positive exact action quantity and agreed unit. Rating refuses missing/incompatible basis instead of assuming one or current subscription quantity. Flat action fees do not multiply by seats; later increases do not change the original action. Distinct additional fees require separately agreed actions.

**SUB-D-44: Structured pending reasons.** Rating's SDK-readable status for an exact pending fact contains a nonempty blocker list: stable code, affected interval/lot/window/dependency, responsible source, evidence and expected resolution route. Subscriptions' unresolved reasons remain source authority; Rating owns its own coverage/recovery findings. Previous complete results identify their own source versions and cannot establish current completion.

**SUB-D-45: Empty, zero and pending.** A complete empty replacement removes only that fact's former monetary contributions through Billing's guarded replacement. An active zero-valued obligation keeps classified zero lines, accepted terms and provenance. Missing conditions/data yields structured pending without complete monetary delivery. Zero usage still needs coverage and applicable minimum valuation; a zero total cannot erase lines or prove refund completion.

**SUB-D-46: Recurring flat and pooled period floor.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

**SUB-D-48: Usage kind and resource group.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

**SUB-D-49: Tariff-selected scope.** Requested of Rating and Pricing under S-U08; the rule and its examples are recorded in [UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#23-rating-and-billing) as `cpt-cf-bss-subscriptions-upreq-valuation-rules` (SUB-D-75).

## Readiness and execution

**SUB-D-55: Initial readiness before active.** Initial usable access precedes local active/T and service-period start. Subscriptions persists the activation operation and preparation posture before dispatch. Entitlements owns the complete immutable required-assignment/producer manifest and reports an aggregate result bound to that exact operation/preparation revision/digest. An explicit empty manifest is required for a no-preparation profile. Subscriptions validates its binding and all final guards, then commits active state, fresh local T, period start, applied receipt and outbox together.

Required initial assignments include account creation/linking, included deployment and usable permissions; first login and unrelated later resource creation are not prerequisites. Late/unknown results remain recoverable, and no readiness result is not proof of non-execution. License Resolver is read/check only; issuing/feed belongs to the selected execution owner. Renewal, guarded resume, conversion and active modifications retain their separate contracts. See [the consumer readiness protocol](features/05-entitlements.md#initial-activation-readiness-contract).

**SUB-D-56: Entitlements ownership.** Entitlements owns rule semantics, provider-desired state, readiness manifests, adapter contracts and reconciliation. Subscriptions publishes `CommercialPostureV1` atomically with its source revision and consumes `ReadinessObservationV1`. Pricing stores quota/grant rules on PlanRevision and freezes them in acceptance. Adapter requests carry authorized scope, assignment and desired revision, never `subscriptionId`.

Disposition/hold changes publish complete snapshots in newer posture revisions even without a lifecycle status change; receipt-only persistence does not create a new commercial revision. Allocation preparation uses the authorized operation/scope and purchased capacity, counts real occupied resources before T and retains quota/source/member/allocation identities through T. Platform-borne cost does not erase occupancy. Fenced non-activation stops new allocation; occupied capacity is freed only by confirmed resource release and quota settlement. Shared-resource cleanup requires explicit authority.

Checks can carry `purpose = readiness_check` so providers recognize preparation. The provider selects inspection, dry-run or real execution and its accounting; the marker grants no permission, exemption, free quota or success guarantee. No consuming probe or service allowance is mandatory. Real effects still require idempotency, provenance and recovery. Counter/window identity survives activation. The current quota scope remains allocation-only; this recognition hook does not enable deferred consumable-limit enforcement.

Purchased capacity remains the desired commercial cap. An independent provider restriction is an observed discrepancy, not a purchase rewrite. Known incompatible offers cannot be accepted/captured; later discovered incompatibility retains the obligation and remediation. Required initial readiness cannot pass merely because a smaller environment works. Observations have their own ordering; no implicit counter reset, eviction or refund follows.

**SUB-D-57: Held readiness and activation resolution.** Entitlements holds prepared assignments under a durable commitment for the exact activation operation/preparation revision/manifest. Subscriptions serializes activation and ordinary withdrawal on one local fence and publishes the immutable terminal resolution through the posture contract. No remote call runs under that local lock. Timeout, absent/unreachable reads or failed guards do not authorize cleanup.

Both `applied(T)` and `closed_without_activation` identify authenticated namespace/scope, operation, preparation revision/digest and decision version. Applied also binds the complete manifest and commitment. Closure works before a manifest/commitment exists or arrives; it carries known bindings, fences late preparation/readiness and lets Entitlements recover all partial or later-discovered effects of that generation. Old closure cannot affect a successor. Applied continues the same assignments; proven non-activation allows only authorized cleanup.

Independently authorized emergency security blocking remains immediate and reports correlated evidence. An already committed activation remains historical truth and follows subsequent guarded lifecycle/financial policy. Held readiness is not an uptime guarantee. Authorized pre-T and never-activated preparation use is platform-borne, creates no ordinary customer service charge and consumes no trial bonus. Profiles bound exposure and supply durable settlement without changing accepted purchase validity or financial admission.

Each attempt snapshots preparation-policy version, authorized start and deadline; waiting for the agreed future start is separate. Expiry/definitive failure closes that attempt at the activation fence; an earlier applied result retains T. Orders remediates the same purchase/payment/claim, without automatic purchase cancellation/refund. Unknown outcome retains effects. Successor admission requires predecessor non-application and safely settled/quarantined effects under generation fences. Retries, restarts and profile edits cannot extend the deadline or bought start constraints.

**SUB-D-58: `preparing` status.** A subscription is `preparing` from the authorized start of its initial preparation to the guarded activation commit. Subscriptions alone writes this status, in the same transaction that persists or closes the activation operation; no provider or Entitlements observation changes it. Closure without activation (deadline, definitive failure, ordinary withdrawal of the attempt) returns the subscription to `draft` and records the reason in the operation's activation resolution; a successor attempt moves it to `preparing` again. Only an explicit subscription cancellation during preparation moves it to `cancelled`, under the SUB-D-57 fence; Orders compensation that withdraws an attempt and then voids the draft passes through `draft`. There is no direct `draft → active` edge.

`preparing` is the only in-flight phase that becomes a status, because it is the only one that holds T, accrual and occupancy conversion (SUB-D-55). Resume and active composition changes keep their status while Entitlements delivers asynchronously. For occupancy a `preparing` subscription counts as a draft; its place is the order reservation. A `draft` with no activation operation has never been prepared; a `draft` with a closed operation was prepared and closed. Replaces the 2026-10-18 rule that kept a preparing subscription in `draft` with its progress visible only through the operation.

**SUB-D-59: Receiver attempt pause.** Each activation attempt is a receiver ledger entry keyed by its acquisition origin `(originType, originId, originVersion, attemptId, generation, lineId)` (SUB-D-71; for Orders: order id, version, attempt, generation, line) with the immutable grant digest and authority and a state `open | paused | revoked | settled`. Admission, the guarded activation commit and the Workflow commands `pause`, `unpause` and `revoke` serialize under the activation operation's local fence, in the fixed lock order with capacity and subscription rows. `pause` (an Orders hold) blocks both new preparation admission and the activation commit for that generation. If pause wins before admission, `draft → preparing` refuses without preparation effects. If preparation already started, pause does not close it, release held readiness or publish a withdrawal, and the subscription stays `preparing`. The preparation deadline stops while paused and continues from the remaining time at `unpause`. A `pause` that arrives after the activation commit is recorded on the drain roster and the subscription stays `active`: an order hold is not a subscription suspension. `revoke` closes the attempt through the ordinary withdrawal path (`preparing → draft`). An exact same-key command replays; a changed payload refuses; a close received before open records a tombstone for that receiver generation. Attempt-wide create settlement additionally uses the stable create identity of SUB-D-33; neither tombstone releases the separate reservation; a higher generation needs a newly authorized Orders grant, never only a larger number. This same-generation `unpause` protocol is a request pending Orders adoption under S-U01. Orders currently resumes with a successor grant and leaves the old generation paused. Such a grant cannot take over existing preparation or reset its remaining deadline in this profile. The owners must agree on this requested resume protocol or explicitly replace it with a preparation-transfer contract before enabling protected acquisition; AP-C14 is required joint evidence.

**SUB-D-60: Fresh execution check before activation.** Before the guarded activation decision, Subscriptions runs the fresh `check_fulfilment` that Orders D-190 requires for the accepted purchase, outside the local lock, and stages its versioned result. The check validates execution of the accepted purchase; it never reprices or replaces accepted bindings. The [execution-check result mapping](DESIGN.md#execution-check-results) covers expiry, explicit price closure/temporary end, SKU retirement, market change, acceptance mismatch and an activation outside the accepted window. A definitive commercial refusal closes the attempt as `closed_without_activation` with its original typed reason (`preparing → draft`); Orders receives the agreed mapping and remediates the retained purchase under S-U01. Authorization, missing evidence, corruption and transport failures keep their own categories. An unavailable Pricing or unknown result cannot authorize activation: the attempt waits within its saved deadline, and deadline closure retains the dependency cause without inventing commercial expiry. Ordinary successor publication or deprecation for new sales alone is not an execution refusal. A durable AP03 commitment requires a versioned Pricing check that explicitly accepts and validates that commitment; Subscriptions cannot ignore a V1 refusal or extend V1 `hold_until`. Missing provider support or result mapping keeps the profile gated before capture. The staged result is rechecked for freshness in the commit like every other observed dependency version; it is not a reusable admission token.

**SUB-D-61: Change-delta acceptance and purchase origin.** A quantity increase or component addition is a new purchase and needs its own accepted delta evidence from Pricing under the S-U16 contract: keyed by the change order, version and line plus the target subscription and the original revision the request is expressed against, carrying the added quantity or component with bindings pinned at the catalog version current at change-order submit. Its frozen instant is the commit of the applied change, not an activation instant; the contract defines its hold window and expiry. A retry after a definitively unapplied attempt keeps the same acceptance and purchase terms. If the expected revision changes, Pricing must supply immutable revalidation evidence binding that acceptance, its digest, the target subscription and the new expected revision. Revalidation checks that the same delta remains applicable; it neither reprices the purchase nor extends its hold or expiry. Missing or refused revalidation blocks the retry under S-U16. Acquisition acceptance (`NewSaleQuery`, `hold`) is not reused for a delta. Until S-U16 is delivered, order-originated increases and additions stay gated. A direct or operator wrapper makes no purchase in this version: it has no accepted delta to execute and no Orders approval or booking, so an increase or addition through a wrapper refuses before effects; wrappers remain for non-purchase operations. Standalone acquisition stays deferred for the same reason.

**SUB-D-62: Change purchase identity and attempts.** The purchase identity of an increase or addition is the change order, version and line: it maps to one lot, one obligation and one S-U16 acceptance, and its payload digest excludes the expected revision. An execution attempt is that identity plus the Orders attempt id and the expected aggregate revision. An exact repeat of an attempt replays its outcome; a changed purchase payload conflicts. A new attempt is admitted only after its predecessor is definitively unapplied (refused, or closed without application); an unknown predecessor outcome is recovered first. The new attempt carries a new expected revision and the same purchase identity, reuses the same delta acceptance with S-U16 revalidation for its new revision, and applies at most once; a delayed predecessor is fenced by its attempt id and cannot apply after a successor. S-U01 association and settlement apply only to the draft-change profile, not to active-subscription changes.

**SUB-D-63: Occupancy claim set.** A subscription holds one claim per distinct occupancy key `(payerTenantId, resourceTenantId, overlapScopeKey)` that its resulting composition maps to: its claim set. Confirmation reserves, and activation converts, every key of the accepted composition. A component addition atomically claims the new keys of the resulting composition without releasing any existing key; a conflict on any new key refuses the whole change and preserves the old composition and claim set. A key leaves the set only when its component leaves the composition, which is deferred, so in this version the set only grows. Suspend retains, resume converts and applied cancel releases the whole set; a lowered limit never evicts a claim. The occupancy read returns the key set a subscription or order line holds, so Orders can refuse an addition whose new key is held by another subscription before approval. Migration of an existing component's key stays gated under S-Q02/S-U10. Replaces the single-place wording and the claim move for additions.

**SUB-D-64: Cancellation guard on purchases.** A seat purchase, quantity increase or component addition refuses before effects while the target subscription has a pending or unresolved failed cancellation intent of any kind (immediate, end-of-term or future-date), and after a confirmed charging cutoff, whether that cutoff has passed or is scheduled. The guard is checked at submission and again in the completing transaction, next to the status guard. A customer who wants to buy more on a subscription with a scheduled cancellation first withdraws (unschedules) that intent; a purchase that ends at a future cutoff is not supported in this version. The matrix in feature 03 keys on status and on this guard; status alone is not admission.

**SUB-D-65: Conversion lead time.** A scheduled prepay conversion starts its prescribed collection at `E − conversionLeadTime`, where E is the original trial expiry and the lead time is part of the accepted trial terms on the PlanRevision (S-U12). Collection must reach a definitive outcome, applied or failed, before E. If the outcome is still unknown at E, the episode ends at E exactly as a normal expiry: access is revoked, no paid period starts and no debt arises. A capture that succeeds after E does not revive the closed episode (FA-C08); it is remediated as an erroneous collection by refund, or, only with the customer's explicit assent, turned into a new purchase through the ordinary route. Postpay conversion has no collection and is not affected; it needs its authorization before E under the same rule. Pending is therefore never treated as absence of a route and never extends the trial.

**SUB-D-66: Credit exhaustion action.** The accepted trial terms of a credit trial carry `exhaustionAction ∈ {convert, end}` (S-U12). With `convert`, authoritative exhaustion is the third conversion trigger, under the same single-episode guard as early and scheduled conversion: it converts at the exhaustion commit when a paid route is authorized (postpay authority, or a prescribed prepay payment already satisfied); for an accepted prepay route without satisfied payment, revoke trial access and enter `conversion-pending` while starting or recovering one collection. This state preserves data but grants no service or new credit. Conversion may commit only before original expiry and after all guards pass. Definitive payment failure or original expiry closes the pending conversion as `end`; an unknown payment stays recoverable without delaying that closure. A later capture cannot reopen the episode and follows the SUB-D-65 financial remedy or new-purchase route. With `end`, or when no paid route is authorized, exhaustion takes the normal trial end action: access is revoked, data is retained, no debt arises. Exhaustion grants no permission to continue service at platform cost. Actual stopping-delay usage and SUB-D-67 hourly allocation remain platform-borne without granting access or creating customer debt. A missing `exhaustionAction` refuses the credit trial terms.

**SUB-D-67: Hourly-aligned trial boundaries.** A valid early, scheduled or exhaustion conversion records the actual paid transition `T_paid`. Status and access change at that instant. Each component also records its paid rating/allocation boundary `B`: `T_paid` for non-hourly components, or the earliest UTC-hour boundary at or after `T_paid` for hourly-windowed components. At an exact hour `B = T_paid`; no extra free hour is added. Rating and Billing use the same component boundary, not event arrival time.

During the hourly interval `[T_paid, B)`, a free-period component keeps its zero binding. A credit-trial component keeps its previous tariff: eligible expenses may use the same bounded, applicable grant only for service before its original exclusive expiry `E`; the platform bears the remaining value. Exhausted or expired credit covers nothing, and the hourly remainder creates no customer debt. From `B`, accepted paid terms apply after any still-valid eligible bonus coverage. Non-hourly components use paid terms from `T_paid`.

Expiry without a valid paid conversion is not an aligned paid transition: access revocation starts at `E`, and no paid boundary or successor binding is invented. Actual stopping-delay usage after `E` remains platform-borne until access stops, uses no trial credit and is not a trial expense. Rounding never extends access, the trial or grant expiry. Trial start retains the ordinary activation rule for hourly lines.

Facts retain `T_paid` when present, each component's `B`, original `E`, actual served extents and the frozen rule version. Hourly tariff slices change at `B`; actual service and eligibility boundaries remain exact. Do not split a scalar hourly peak at `T_paid` or prorate it by minutes. If grant eligibility or stopping-delay allocation needs evidence the provider cannot supply, keep the affected allocation pending under S-U08/S-U15; never invent coverage, customer debt or an expiry extension.

**SUB-D-68: Two grace episodes.** Grace has two kinds with one evaluated ladder (grace, then suspension with nonpayment dwell, then cancellation). A **renewal grace** covers a term that never started: a definitive financial refusal at renewal anchors it at the accepted term boundary, the next-term obligation stays blocked, and a late success completes the same renewal with the term starting at its boundary. A **collection grace** covers a term already renewed or applied whose invoice is due and unpaid: Billing's authoritative due/unpaid outcome anchors it at the due/unpaid effective time and binds it to `(subscriptionId, stable Billing collection-obligation ID)` in authorized payer/tenant scope. The applied term is provenance, not the collection episode key. One renewal grace exists per term occurrence; one collection grace exists per distinct collection obligation, so one term may have several collection episodes. Invoice/fact references and their corrected versions remain ordered evidence on that episode, not new episode identities. Corrections, duplicate failures and retry-exhaustion signals do not restart grace or reset the frozen anchor/deadline. The term is not changed, and resolution of one obligation affects only its own collection episode. Exact obligation identity, correction lineage and outcome ordering require Billing adoption under S-U07. While a collection grace is open the next renewal proceeds under the accepted postpay authority; the open debt is enforced only through its own ladder, which Billing's dunning feeds.

**SUB-D-69: Notice-dependent terms are an admission gate.** Because notification delivery is deferred, no action in this version may depend on a notice at runtime. Acceptance refuses a binding or Contract whose terms require a notice or assent for renewal, continuation or deletion; the renewal route to the versioned platform defaults carries no price-change notice requirement in this version, so a Contract that ends without a successor renews to the defaults without blocking. Acceptance also refuses a binding with a known in-term `ends_on` unless Pricing supplies continuation evidence (a successor binding or an explicit notice-free continuation rule). An `Unresolved` successor fact therefore arises only from a late-discovered boundary and raises an operator-visible finding with its age. Cancellation bounds further service but leaves already served unresolved intervals and their seals pending. Historical resolution requires recovery of authoritative continuation evidence valid for the served interval, followed by a full fact/seal replacement. Neither an operator nor a new purchase may invent historical prices or mark the missing interval free. S-U08/S-U13 must provide an owner-agreed recovery or financial remedy for missing historical authority before this profile is enabled; there is no local `confirmAcceptance` or waiver shortcut. The v1 default retention carries no required notice: the 7-day and 1-day retention notices are deferred scheduling, and their absence does not block deletion. Refines SUB-D-17.

**SUB-D-70: Local no-preparation profile.** A deployment may select the service profile `no_preparation` for subscriptions that need no provider preparation before service. Under it, Subscriptions itself records the explicit empty manifest and its aggregate `ready` observation in the same transaction that persists the authorized activation operation, with the profile version as evidence; no `CommercialPostureV1` is dispatched for preparation and Entitlements is not called. The subscription passes through `preparing` within that transaction and the guarded activation commit follows at once under the same final guards (SUB-D-55 unchanged otherwise). Lifecycle posture after activation still publishes `CommercialPostureV1` when the deployment runs Entitlements; a deployment without Entitlements enables only profiles that need no provider application at all. Every other profile requires Entitlements and its explicit manifest, empty or not.

**SUB-D-71: Acquisition origin.** A subscription is created from an acquisition origin, not from an order as such. `AcquisitionOriginV1` carries the origin type (a GTS instance), the origin identity and version, the attempt id, the generation and the line id; each operation uses the appropriate subset of these fields. The receiver ledger (SUB-D-59) includes generation. Create and its no-create tombstone use origin type/id/version, attempt and line, excluding generation (SUB-D-33). The confirmation reservation and its release tombstone use origin type/id/version and line, excluding both attempt and generation. Change-purchase identity likewise stays stable across attempts as defined by SUB-D-62. Replay and successor recovery must preserve these distinct scopes; the full envelope is not one shared business key. Orders is the first origin provider: `originType = cf.bss.orders` with order id, order version, attempt, generation and line in those fields. An origin provider owns the accepted purchase evidence (S-U01 commitment, S-U16 for changes), the occupancy reservation at confirmation, capture ordering and compensation, the hold, unpause and revoke commands and the remediation of a closed attempt; another origin type (marketplace, checkout, storefront, external CPQ or ERP) is enabled only with an agreed adapter contract that supplies these and executed conformance evidence. In this version only the Orders origin is enabled; a create from an unregistered or disabled origin refuses before effects, and enabling an origin later changes no schema.

**SUB-D-72: Policy set in Settings Service.** The lifecycle policy defaults form one GTS-typed policy set, `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.policy_set.v1~`: grace, nonpayment dwell, retention, archive dwell, hold-release policy, preparation duration per service profile, abandoned-draft TTL, the occupancy `maxConcurrentActive` and scope-key rule, and the approved bounds for order overrides. Settings Service stores the values as a settings category of that type, authorizes changes and resolves the effective value through its tenant scope hierarchy; Subscriptions reads it through `SettingsClientV1` and freezes the resolved value with its version and provenance at evaluation, as every frozen deadline already requires. Precedence is platform, then seller tenant (Settings Service scopes), then plan (an override carried by the PlanRevision, Pricing), then Contract (S-U06), then order values within the approved bounds. A legal or governance minimum is a constraint that the owning governance service supplies to Settings Service; Subscriptions refuses an effective value below it. In this version platform and seller-tenant scopes are live through Settings Service (S-U17); plan and Contract overrides stay behind their gates. The platform values of this design (7, 90, 30 and 90 days) are the initial platform-scope settings, not literals in code.

**SUB-D-73: Extension points.** Three vendor extension points are part of the contract surface. (1) Cancellation reasons are well-known instances of `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.cancel_reason.v1~`; each carries the required traits `financialClass ∈ {early_termination_eligible, no_fee, correction}` and `initiator ∈ {customer, platform}`. The six reasons of this design are platform instances, and a vendor registers its own (fraud, compliance, reseller-initiated, migration) with the same traits; every rule that depends on a reason reads its traits, never its name. (2) Lifecycle hooks have two points on every lifecycle operation: `before_apply` runs after preliminary guards, outside the transaction and before acquiring commit locks, on an immutable versioned snapshot, may only allow or veto with a typed reason, has a time budget from the operation profile, performs no write and no financial or resource effect, and fails closed (timeout or error refuses the operation as `dependency_unavailable`); the completing transaction rechecks the snapshot revision, evidence and hook/profile versions, and stale results require a fresh evaluation outside locks; `after_applied` is delivered from the outbox at least once with the idempotency key `(operationId, revision)` and cannot change the outcome. A hook is a plugin instance of `gts.cf.toolkit.plugins.plugin.v1~cf.bss.subscriptions.lifecycle_hook.v1~` that declares its operations and point and runs on the platform serverless runtime or task engine. The engine carries the hook stage and the identifiers from the first increment; executing hooks is delivered behind gate S-U18, until which the registry is empty and the stage is a no-op. (3) The aggregate has a typed `attributes` slot of a vendor-derived type under `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.attributes.v1~`, versioned with the revision, readable on every event and read, and never consulted by a commercial rule; it cannot carry or rebind tenant axes. The trial union and the suspend/resume checks stay closed: they are commercial semantics with money and gates (S-U12, S-U07), not vocabularies.

**SUB-D-74: Adapter plugins and resolved profiles.** The financial-admission, historical-inventory, usage-coverage and lifecycle-hook seams are ToolKit plugins: a GTS plugin specification registered by this gear, a public and a plugin trait in the SDK, one GTS instance per adapter with a scoped ClientHub client, and resolution through the types registry. Each plugin trait exposes `capabilities()`, read at startup and on configuration change. Selection is by vendor in gear configuration, with a per-seller-tenant override through the policy set for financial admission and usage coverage. A profile is never declared by hand: it is resolved as the intersection of the installed instances' declared capabilities, the resolved policy set and the closed gates, recorded as a versioned `ProfileResolutionV1`; an operation records the profile version under which it was admitted, and a capability without its instance, declaration or closed gate refuses as `capability_closed`.

**SUB-D-75: Owner rules are requests, not decisions.** Valuation rules (recurring flat, minimum fees, windows, partial hours and periods: the former bodies of SUB-D-46 to SUB-D-54) and the trial-credit accounting rules (grant issuance, allocation, same-grant correction, retention after early conversion, platform-borne excess) belong to Rating, Pricing and Billing. This design keeps only what Subscriptions publishes and consumes (fact shape, term slices, lot allocation, evidence references, the Rating result contract) and records the expected owner behaviour as requested requirements in UPSTREAM_REQS §2.3 (`upreq-valuation-rules` under S-U08, `upreq-trial-credit-accounting` under S-U15), with their examples. A request becomes binding when the owner ratifies it in its own register; until then the affected profile stays gated. The texts are offered to Rating, Pricing and the future Billing register through owner pull requests.

**SUB-D-76: Audit record.** The answer to who changed a subscription, when and why does not depend on the event broker. Every applied or refused operation writes one audit record, `gts.cf.bss.subscriptions.audit.v1~`, in the same transaction as its operation record and revision snapshot: subscription, revision before and after, operation identity and kind, asserting principal and delegation evidence, reason instance where the operation carries one, correlation, status before and after, T where applied, outcome and refusal reason. It is a projection over records this design already keeps, not a second copy of state. An authorized read `audit_since` returns it by subscription or ordering tenant under the scope, cursor and continuation rules of the recovery reads, and its retention follows the financially referenced history. Domain events remain the feed for external consumers; a platform audit consumer, when one exists, reads `audit_since`. A cross-gear audit subsystem with query and export stays deferred.

**SUB-D-77: REST projection.** The REST surface is a projection of the same `OperationBuilder` operations the SDK exposes under `/subscriptions/v1`: list and read with OData filtering and the revision as `ETag`, composition and audit reads, operation status, one action endpoint per lifecycle operation, the receiver ledger commands for Orders Workflow, and the recovery reads of feature 09. Existing-subscription mutations carry `If-Match` with the expected revision. Create and receiver commands instead carry the full authorized acquisition-origin tuple and serialize under its durable receiver fence; they require no nonexistent aggregate ETag. Receiver pause/unpause/revoke/settle and exact lookup work before creation. All mutations require an `Idempotency-Key`; create returns 201, an applied action or recovered result 200, and pending work 202 with a status path. Errors are RFC 9457 problems from the §4.2 registry. The OpenAPI document is produced with the code from this resource model, so REST and SDK cannot drift.

The [provider register](UPSTREAM_REQS.md#25-provider-prerequisites-and-bilateral-acceptance) names capability gates. An adapter that cannot satisfy the contract keeps that profile disabled.

**Metered-usage allocation.** A usage component may be added, or its included allowance increased, when the meter scope is the whole subscription and the whole component keeps one accepted binding. An increase that needs allocation of metered usage or tier qualification between purchase lots or between subscriptions refuses before effects until the S-U08 quantity-lot allocation contract covers metered usage.

Financial admission binds the exact acquisition/operation and accepted terms. Initial activation consumes the retained allocated payment or durable future postpay commitment, without another debit or credit assessment. Technical proof expiry and later risk/limit changes do not revoke that basis. Explicit applicable financial dispositions are versioned and fenced against local application; missing evidence is recovered under the same acquisition. Ledger accounting is evidence, not admission authority.

Orders Lifecycle owns durable capture-start arbitration against acquisition cancellation after place and terms are protected. Workflow transports and recovers; Payments deduplicates the stable capture identity. Cancellation winning prevents late debit. Capture winning preserves cancellation/remedy rights. Unknown capture keeps protection; release requires authoritative resolution and capture/activation fences with the applicable remedy, not an assumed refund amount or timing. Multi-line outcomes retain per-line obligations; successful lines are not automatically cancelled by another line's failure.

Explicit compensation has its own authorization and evidence.
